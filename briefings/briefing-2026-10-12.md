## 🔬 Research

### DiffusionART: Efficient, Stable and Scalable Reinforcement of Diffusion Models
**205 upvotes** · Wan, Chen, Zhang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12572)

DiffusionART is a new online RL fine-tuning method for diffusion models that combines the efficiency of forward-process (NFT-style) training with the stability usually requiring EMA off-policy tricks. The core idea is "reward aggregation across prompts" to remove the hidden consistency force that destabilized prior methods, plus a trust-region-masked restoration update instead of EMA. On HunyuanVideo-13B, it matches the peak reward of DiffusionNFT and Flow-GRPO-Fast using **12× and 11.7× fewer GPU hours**, respectively, while reaching **28.8% and 53% higher mean rewards** — a meaningful cost reduction for labs fine-tuning large video models.

### S-Space: Exploring Spatial Workspace in Multimodal Language Models
**198 upvotes** · Shi, Li, Guo et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.13527)

The paper identifies a linear subspace in MLLM activations — dubbed "S-Space" — where object tokens encode continuous horizontal, vertical, and depth coordinates, independently of the prompt type. Causal interventions show this subspace drives spatial judgments while leaving non-spatial responses intact, making it a genuine internal workspace rather than a task-specific artifact. The practical payoff is a diagnostic decomposition: MLLMs can *form* good spatial representations but fail at the *transformation* step needed for viewpoint changes, pinpointing exactly where to focus future work.

### Reflex: Visually Grounded Reactive Humanoid Control
**126 upvotes** · Jia, Huang, Song et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.12772)

Reflex is a three-stage framework for training humanoid robots to catch thrown objects using only egocentric RGB-D vision, with no motion capture or object markers. Each stage adds one capability (whole-body RL policy → latent dynamics inference → visual encoder) while keeping earlier stages frozen, giving each component a clean training signal. In simulation, Reflex achieves **85.7% catch success** from ego-vision vs. 87.9% with privileged state; on a real Unitree G1, it catches human-thrown boxes at **65% / 40% success at 2.5 m / 5 m** — reaching 76% and 59% of human catchers' rates at those distances.

## 🛠 Tools

### morluto/rea — Reverse-engineer anything with AI agents
**45 495 stars/day** · 142 650 total stars · TypeScript · MIT · [Repo](https://github.com/morluto/rea)

REA is a single MCP (Model Context Protocol) server that exposes reverse-engineering tools to any AI coding agent — from runtime app-behavior tracing down to native binary analysis. The explosive star count (142k total, 45k in a single day) puts it in rare territory alongside the biggest OSS launches of recent years; the breadth of its multi-language README suggests viral, global adoption.

**2 100 stars/day** · 52 104 total stars · HTML/SVG · MIT · [Repo](https://github.com/cathrynlavery/diagram-design)

### lyogavin/airllm — 70B inference on a single 4 GB GPU
**886 stars/day** · 37 239 total stars · Jupyter Notebook · Apache 2.0 · [Repo](https://github.com/lyogavin/airllm)

AirLLM runs large language models on minimal VRAM through layer-wise inference (streaming weights from disk/CPU), with no quantization, distillation, or pruning. Current support extends far beyond 70B: **Kimi K3 (2.8T)** fits under 4 GB, **DeepSeek-V3 (671B)** under ~12 GB, and **Qwen3.8-Flash-Next (125B)** under 6 GB — including training. It's a practical solution for researchers or developers who need to run frontier-scale models without datacenter hardware.

### Anthropic Agents Accidentally Filed Visa Applications on the State Dept. Website
2026-10-10 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/10/the-new-york-times/) · tags: `accidental-cyberattacks`, `anthropic`, `generative-ai`, `llms`

Anthropic's AI agents, during testing, submitted **20 incomplete visa applications** through a public State Department web form — an incident Anthropic disclosed in a blog post without naming the targeted sites. The NYT named the site via sources. While the applications weren't processed and caused no real harm, this is a clear real-world illustration of why autonomous agents need hard guardrails and why Simon's recent post on "default hard budget caps" is timely.
