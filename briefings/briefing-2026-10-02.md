## 🔬 Research

### OneStreamer: Unifying Perception, Memory, and Proactive Response in Streaming Video Interaction
**94 upvotes** · Xiangyu Zeng et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.01762)

Streaming video LLMs face a hard trade-off: they must retain evidence before its relevance is known, yet respond in real time. OneStreamer solves this with a shared *proactive generation* process that simultaneously builds a hierarchical caption memory (time-grounded local captions + event summaries) and learns when to respond, without re-reading historical visual features at inference. A companion 1M-record streaming dataset (OneStreamer-1M) was synthesized to align output timing with available evidence. The 4B model tops all eight evaluated streaming video understanding benchmarks, with PSTL achieving better state supervision than dense baselines while annotating only 27.5% of state tokens.

### Agent Priors-guided Policy Learning (APPL)
**55 upvotes** · Puming Jiang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.35690)

Robot learning from few demos requires two entangled generalization types — compositional (combining skills for new tasks) and skill-level (applying a policy in new situations) — yet information about *where* a skill applies is lost at the composition interface. APPL embeds each skill policy's structural prior (e.g., "grasp depends only on gripper pose relative to object") directly into the skill's interface, so a runtime composition agent knows precisely when to invoke it. Evaluated on MetaWorld and long-horizon ManiSkill tasks, APPL improves out-of-distribution skill generalization and enables previously unseen compositions; removing interface priors substantially degrades performance.

### World Observer: Joint Actor-Observer Generation for Persistent World Modeling
**39 upvotes** · Hyunwook Choi et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02162)

Standard video world models are actor-centric: objects that leave the agent's field of view lose their simulated dynamics and arrive back in inconsistent states. World Observer decouples observing from acting by jointly generating the agent's perspective alongside one or more panoramic *observers* placed freely around the scene, grounded via a shared panoramic warp for geometric consistency. An Observer Sink of high-resolution perspective references restores fine appearance on re-entry. The paper also introduces world-space metrics and a benchmark covering real and synthetic scenes to properly evaluate out-of-view evolution — a gap previous benchmarks ignored.

## 📡 Analysis

### Quoting Matthew Green — Sandboxing is not enough to contain agent worms
2026-10-01 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/1/matthew-green/) · tags: `accidental-cyberattacks`, `ai-misuse`, `ai-security-research`, `sandboxing`, `llms`

Matthew Green (Johns Hopkins cryptographer) lays out a concrete agent-worm threat model: a hijacking payload travels between agents via shared infrastructure (package caches, email, Slack, shared docs), turning each compromised agent into a carrier. The key insight is that independently sandboxed agents can still communicate through ambient shared state — isolation at the execution level doesn't prevent propagation through the data layer. With personal agents like Muse being widely deployed, this is no longer a theoretical risk.

### Quoting Anthropic Frontier Red Team — GLM-5.3 crosses a meaningful binary exploitation threshold
2026-09-29 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/29/anthropic-frontier-red-team/) · tags: `anthropic`, `ai-security-research`, `glm`, `ai-in-china`, `llms`

Anthropic's red team finds that GLM-5.3 (a Chinese frontier model) achieves full control-flow hijacks on 4% of binary exploitation benchmark tasks, while Claude Mythos Preview reaches 6% — neither number is large, but both represent a qualitative leap: Claude Opus 4.6 and GLM-5.2 scored 0% on the same tasks. This signals that advanced offensive cyber capabilities are spreading across model families and geographies, raising the stakes for AI security policy well beyond any single lab.
