## 🔬 Research

### StreamEval: Evaluating Interactive Streaming Video Generation Models
**117 upvotes** · StreamEval Team · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.28402)

StreamEval is the first unified benchmark for interactive, real-time streaming video generation, covering five tasks (avatar dialogue, world generation, instruction editing, etc.) across 300 scripted test cases and 37 models. The key finding is sobering: visual quality and identity retention do not equate to reliable execution — the best "Following" score for avatar-performance models is only 36.90/100, and reference-editing tops out at 15.00/100. This puts hard numbers on how far current models are from being genuinely controllable in real time, making StreamEval a useful yardstick for anyone building interactive video pipelines. Leaderboard: [streameval.ai](https://streameval.ai).

### SpatialScout: Can VLM Agents Actively Explore and Map 3D Scenes?
**101 upvotes** · Zhang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.26659)

SpatialScout benchmarks VLM agents on active 3D object mapping: given a set of objects to find, agents must control a camera to explore 80 indoor scenes (3,140 targets) and estimate object positions in a shared coordinate frame. Even the best-evaluated model, Claude Opus 5.5, achieves only a 41.71% mean overlap score, with just 3.25% of predictions hitting IoU > 0.9. This exposes a fundamental gap between coarse spatial awareness and the precise metric mapping needed for real embodied AI agents. Code is available at [github.com/JesseZZZZZ/SpatialScout](https://github.com/JesseZZZZZ/SpatialScout).

### Looking Ahead: Modeling Future Evolution in Autoregressive Video Generation
**84 upvotes** · Gao et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29989)

The paper identifies a shortcut problem in autoregressive video diffusion: under standard teacher forcing, models can exploit temporal redundancy to match adjacent frames without actually learning how scenes evolve. The fix — Future Chunk Prediction (FCP) — adds an auxiliary objective that forces the model to predict temporally distant chunks with no access to intervening frames, using no extra encoder and not changing inference. FCP improves physical plausibility at a lower compute budget, and visualizations suggest it also produces a more structured latent space, a promising stepping stone toward proper world models.

## 🛠 Tools

### cloudflare/security-audit-skill — Multi-phase AI security auditing agent skill
**2,979 stars/day** · ⭐ 38,178 total · [Repo](https://github.com/cloudflare/security-audit-skill)

This is the single-repo starting point of Cloudflare's internal vulnerability discovery harness, now open-sourced as a reusable coding-agent skill. It orchestrates isolated sub-agents through six structured phases — reconnaissance, coverage-led hunting, candidate validation, structured output, independent record verification, and target-neutral reporting — and outputs machine-readable `findings.json` validated against a JSON schema. Multiple runs on the same repo are additive, and every finding is independently re-verified by a fresh agent before being marked `confirmed`. Practical, opinionated, and directly battle-tested at Cloudflare scale.

### DietrichGebert/ponytail — Minimal-code-first constraint layer for AI agents
**1,949 stars/day** · ⭐ 172,657 total · [Repo](https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail)

Ponytail injects a "laziest senior dev" heuristic into AI coding agents: before writing new code, the agent is prompted to look for existing solutions (stdlib, dependencies already in the project, one-liners) that make writing anything at all unnecessary. It works with 20 agents (Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, etc.), is available on npm as `@dietrichgebert/ponytail`, and is MIT-licensed. With 172K total stars it is clearly resonating, particularly among teams trying to contain AI-generated code bloat.

### Graphify-Labs/graphify — Codebase-to-knowledge-graph skill for AI coding assistants
**1,407 stars/day** · ⭐ 132,339 total · [Repo](https://github.com/Graphify-Labs/graphify)

Graphify parses any codebase — source files, SQL schemas, configs, PDFs, docs — into a queryable knowledge graph using deterministic local AST parsing, no vector store or embeddings required. It integrates as a `/graphify` skill in Claude Code, Cursor, Codex, and Gemini CLI, with every graph edge carrying an explanation of the relationship. The no-vector-store approach means results are reproducible and auditable, which is a meaningful differentiator for teams concerned about hallucinated context in RAG-based alternatives.

### eMudhra Intermediate CA Compromise
*2026-10-20* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/20/emudhra-certificates/) · tags: `encryption`, `https`, `pip`, `python`, `security`, `uv`

The private keys of eight eMudhra intermediate CA certificates were compromised; they chain to roots trusted by Mozilla, Microsoft, Apple, and Chrome, meaning forged TLS certificates for any domain are theoretically possible until trust stores are updated. Python tooling has already shipped fixes: `certifi 2026.10.19`, `pip 26.2.2`, and `uv 0.13.4` all carry the updated certificate lists. Any system — especially CI/CD pipelines and containers — that bundles its own CA list needs an immediate update to avoid trusting fraudulent certificates.
