## 🔬 Research

### Does Learning Protein Folding Generalize to Broader Reasoning?
**108 upvotes** · Yong Liu et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38879)

The authors build **FoldingCorpus**, a protein-derived QA dataset, and post-train a language model (Fold2Reason, based on Qwen3.5-9B) using both discrete structural answers and continuous 3D geometry signals. On structure prediction, Fold2Reason scores 2.7–3.5× better than the base model, but the key finding is broader: it also improves performance on **all 10** spatial, graph, scientific, and general reasoning benchmarks (+3.23 pp macro-average accuracy). This demonstrates that non-linguistic, structure-dense scientific data can serve as a scalable and cheap source of post-training supervision — no human annotation required, just solved physics.

### Scaling Trajectories for Complex Tasks through Recursive Self-Rewrite
**73 upvotes** · Zongxia Li et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.02826)

**Recursive Self-Rewrite (RSR)** uses Qwen-3.8-27B to solve terminal tasks across three different agent harnesses, then rewrites those trajectories into a unified format for supervised fine-tuning. Starting from 2,001 source trajectories, RSR generates 11,094 rewritten ones, boosting pass@3 on Terminal-Bench 2 from 57.0% to 74.2%, and on the harder Terminal-Bench 4 from 1.5% to 9.1%. The practical implication: agent capabilities acquired with specialized tooling can be distilled into a general-purpose harness, making deployment significantly easier.

### FrameMorrow: Future-guided Frame Selection for Long-Horizon Video Generation
**71 upvotes** · Bo Yin et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.38839)

Long video generation suffers from bloated, redundant context as generation history grows. **FrameMorrow** flips the selection criterion: instead of picking frames relevant to the *current* step, it predicts compact "prospective tokens" representing *future* information needs, then uses them to retrieve the most relevant historical frames. It is model-agnostic and works plug-and-play with closed-source generators. Evaluated across 5 benchmarks and 11 generative models, it consistently improves long-range consistency, visual quality, and action alignment.

### We're going to need default hard budget caps on pretty much everything
*2026-10-03* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/3/default-hard-budget-caps/) · tags: `amazon-web-services`, `ai`, `coding-agents`

Simon argues that coding agents spinning up cloud services on autopilot make runaway spend a real, mainstream risk — and that **hard** budget caps (cut-off + errors, not just warning emails) need to be the default behavior on all pay-per-use APIs. Encouragingly, AWS launched spending limits in mid-September 2026 and Google Cloud shipped "Spend Caps" in July, suggesting the industry is already moving this way. The proposed UX: keep the cap on by default, and require an explicit opt-in checkbox to remove it.
