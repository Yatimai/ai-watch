## 🔬 Research

### Training Object Permanence in World Models
**202 upvotes** · Haotian Zhang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28654)

The authors introduce WROP, a 1.5M-sample benchmark of 150 cognitive-science-inspired tasks (covering object permanence, solidity, and related priors) built with Blender-generated synthetic data. They evaluate 14 video generation models on a 300-question exam and show most fail at these basic physical reasoning tasks. Their 16B model PWM-WROP, trained on this corpus, ranks first among continuation models in a blind Elo study. All data, weights, exam scores, and a native-PyTorch training stack on AWS Trainium2 are released publicly.

### SpeakerMem-R1: Speaker-Centered Dual-Track Memory for Multi-Party Dialogue
**83 upvotes** · Haobo Zheng et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.26780)

SpeakerMem-R1 tackles two fundamental weaknesses of LLM memory in multi-party conversations: knowing *who said what* and tracking how relationships evolve over time. It stores both verbatim speaker-labeled messages and derived relational states in separate tracks, queried jointly at inference time. The system achieves state-of-the-art on EverMemBench (62.33%), and a custom RL training method (GRPO with SpeakerLevenshtein reward) raises the base writer's accuracy from 57.4% to 68.2% — a meaningful gain for locally deployable conversational agents.

### Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs
**73 upvotes** · Pavel Tikhonov et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29845)

The paper proposes the "Superposition Linearity Hypothesis": when two text streams are linearly combined at the input, a Transformer outputs a superposition of both next-token distributions. Surprisingly, this linearity appears intrinsic to the architecture and actually *weakens* with more pretraining, but can be restored with lightweight fine-tuning. The practical payoff is a guided decoding procedure that disentangles the two streams in a single forward pass — potentially useful for parallel generation, speculative decoding, or interpretability research.

## 🛠 Tools

### vectorize-io/hindsight — Agent Memory That Learns
**4,463 stars/day** · 35,283 total stars · [Repo](https://github.com/vectorize-io/hindsight)

Hindsight is an MIT-licensed Python library for giving AI agents memory that improves over time, rather than just replaying conversation history. Unlike simple RAG-based recall, it focuses on extracting and generalizing learnings across sessions, with benchmarks and a cloud-hosted option. A companion paper (arXiv:2512.12818) backs the approach, and it integrates across multiple frameworks via a documented SDK.

---

### debpalash/VoiceStudio — Fully-local ElevenLabs alternative
**3,060 stars/day** · [Repo](https://github.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio)

VoiceStudio bundles voice cloning, voice design, video dubbing, dictation, transcription, and audiobook creation — all locally, in 646 languages — into a single open-source Python app. The pitch is a full offline replacement for ElevenLabs, relevant to anyone concerned about cost, privacy, or vendor lock-in. Its breadth of features (dubbing + transcription + audiobook in one tool) makes it practical for content pipelines that need speech at scale.

### paperclipai/paperclip — Open-source agent orchestration for teams
**2,527 stars/day** · 88,529 total stars · [Repo](https://github.com/paperclipai/paperclip)

Paperclip is a Node.js + React dashboard for running *teams* of AI agents as if they were a company: org charts, budgets, goal alignment, and cost tracking in one UI. The analogy from the README is sharp — "If OpenClaw is an employee, Paperclip is the company." With 88k stars and active daily pushes, it's one of the most traction-heavy open-source agent orchestration projects right now. MIT licensed.
