### Waypoint-OPD: Transferring RL-Induced Policy Shifts via Gated On-Policy Distillation
**660 upvotes** · Bingxi Hou et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.21601)

Standard policy-shift distillation copies the difference between a teacher's pre- and post-RL log probabilities onto a student model, but ignores whether that shift was *consistent* throughout training — early and late shifts can partially cancel each other out, polluting the signal. Waypoint-OPD fixes this by introducing an intermediate teacher checkpoint to compare early vs. late directional agreement, and gates the endpoint shift accordingly, suppressing it when the two stages disagree. Tested on Qwen3-4B teachers with 8B, 14B, and 30B-A3B students across math and code, the method tops all six domain–student settings with only one extra frozen-teacher scoring pass. The gating mechanism ablations confirm the gain is real: removing or shuffling the gates measurably hurts reasoning performance.

### MJev-Spatial: System One Spatial Intelligence via Native Attention Matching
**349 upvotes** · Luozheng Qin et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22276)

VLMs currently waste autoregressive decoding on spatial queries whose answer space is tiny (a relation label, a distance scalar, a 2D point). MJev-Spatial reframes all three task types as classification, scoring candidates directly via the backbone's existing query/key projections — no new parameters, no token generation. Built on Qwen3.5-4B and trained on 12.3 M QA pairs, it scores 65.64% overall across 12 benchmarks, ranking first on 7 of them, while running **2.5× faster than the fastest autoregressive baseline** and **5.6× faster than the base model**. The 30.8% training throughput gain from length-aware global batching is an additional practical bonus for anyone reproducing this at scale.

### OpenGCUA: Toward a Fully Open Stack for Generalist Computer Use
**315 upvotes** · Hanzhang Zhou et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.22200)

As frontier computer-use agents (GPT-6 Astra, Claude Opus 5) remain largely closed, OpenGCUA is an open-source, full-stack generalist computer-use agent designed for community study. Its key design choices are code execution (letting intermediate results drive subsequent actions) and harness-to-model decision transfer (shifting task-state logic from fixed rules to the model). On OSWorld-v2, the framework boosts mean partial score by **+19.6 points** and cuts reported steps per task by **53%** across all eight evaluated models. A notable side finding: Qwen3.8-Max uses **19.4× more reasoning tokens** than GPT-6 Astra on real macOS tasks, illustrating how far efficiency varies between frontier models in agentic settings.

### Chirp Pong — P2P Multiplayer via Audio Chirps, Built with Claude
Oct 15, 2026 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/15/chirp-pong/) · tags: `game-design`, `claude-code`, `generative-ai`

Simon Willison built a two-player Pong variant where networking runs entirely over audio: game state is encoded as chirps picked up by the Web Microphone API, requiring no server or WebRTC. Built as a single-prompt experiment using Claude Opus 5.5 Extra in Claude Code, it works across two separate phones. It's a neat proof-of-concept for unconventional peer-to-peer channels, and a reminder that agentic coding tools are lowering the bar for creative, throwaway engineering experiments.
