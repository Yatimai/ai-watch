## 🔬 Research

### Repo-To-Skill: Distilling GitHub Repositories Into AI4AI Skills
**512 upvotes** · Jianlyu Chen et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.02749)

The paper introduces **DisCo**, a research agent that distills "operational knowledge" — the practical know-how embedded in ML repos and papers — into compact, reusable **skills**. Applied at scale, this yields the **AREX-Skill Library**: 5,000+ verified skills extracted from 1,000 ML repositories across 20 areas. With a fixed GPT-5.5 backbone, adding these skills boosts performance by **+134.3% on MLE-bench** and +34.4% on PaperBench, suggesting that structured operational context is the key missing layer in autonomous research agents.

### HarnessDev: Can LLMs Create and Evolve Their Own Agent Harness?
**234 upvotes** · Yuhao Wu et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.01437)

**HarnessDev** is a benchmark that shifts evaluation focus from task outputs to the agent's ability to build and improve its own execution infrastructure (the "harness"). Results across 6 LLMs and 2,207 downstream instances show that LLM-generated harnesses still lag behind mature human-engineered references on code and research tasks, though they match or exceed them on writing and ML experimentation. Iterative self-evolution helps somewhat but remains unstable and doesn't transfer well across models — a clear signal that harness quality is a meaningful, under-studied bottleneck.

---

### Aspire: Can Models Self-Evolve from Vague Goals?
**210 upvotes** · Yuhao Wu et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.31111)

**ASPIRE** tests a harder variant of self-evolution: agents receive only a natural-language goal (e.g. "improve at research") with no tasks or metrics specified, and must figure out what and how to learn. Evaluated on 520 hidden expert-authored items across 6 goals, current agents complete training loops but show sparse and unstable weight-level improvements, and the best evolved harness still lags behind a hand-engineered Qwen-Agent baseline. The benchmark surfaces a critical failure mode: agents train on mismatched data and over-trust narrow self-evaluations, causing local gains to not generalize.

## 🛠 Tools

### mattpocock/skills — Composable agent skills for real engineering
**2,757 stars/day** · ⭐ 249,294 total · [Repo](https://github.com/mattpocock/skills)

Matt Pocock (of Total TypeScript fame) publishes the agent skill files from his own `.agents` directory as a public, installable library. Skills are intentionally small and composable — the philosophy is deliberate control over the agent's process rather than handing it over to monolithic frameworks like BMAD or GSD. Install via a Claude Code plugin (managed, auto-updating) or via `skills.sh` (editable local copies); works with any model.

---

### DietrichGebert/ponytail — Minimal-effort agent scaffolding
**1,683 stars/day** · ⭐ 124,727 total · [Repo](https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail)

Ponytail is a JavaScript library that nudges AI agents toward the laziest-but-correct solution: reuse existing code, avoid writing net-new logic, prefer deletions over additions. It integrates with 20 agent runtimes via an npm package and is MIT-licensed. In a landscape full of tools that push agents to generate more code, this is a notable counter-thesis — and its star count suggests it's resonating.

---

### debpalash/VoiceStudio — Fully-local ElevenLabs alternative
**1,345 stars/day** · ⭐ 17,136 total · [Repo](https://github.com/DietrichGebert/ponytail)

VoiceStudio (formerly OmniVoice-Studio) offers voice cloning, video dubbing, dictation, transcription, and audiobook generation across **646 languages**, running entirely on local hardware with no API key or subscription required. It bundles **16 TTS engines and 11 ASR engines**, and supports macOS, Windows, Linux, and Docker. A strong practical alternative for teams with privacy constraints or who want to avoid per-character pricing.
