## 🔬 Research

### Training Object Permanence in World Models
**209 upvotes** · Zhang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.28654)

WROP is a dataset of 1.5M samples built from 150 cognitive-science-inspired tasks (10,000+ samples each) designed to teach video generation models object permanence and solidity — core human physical intuitions. The authors evaluate 14 video models and find their 16B model, PWM-WROP, ranks first among continuation models in a blind Elo study. The full data, exam, model weights, and a native-PyTorch training stack targeting AWS Trainium2 are all released, making this a practical benchmark and training pipeline for physical world models.

### FuseReg: Regularizing Layer Fusion Mitigates the Reconstruction-Generation Gap in Representation Autoencoders
**111 upvotes** · Du et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.31620)

Representation autoencoders face a hard trade-off: shallow encoder layers preserve pixel detail, deep layers help generation quality, and fixing which layers to fuse hurts one or the other. FuseReg trains over random subsets of encoder layers, forcing robustness to any fusion choice — a single decoder can then handle full, sparse, or single-layer fusions without retraining. The gains are concrete: PSNR improves over fixed-fusion baselines, and unguided gFID drops 27% with just a decoder swap, or 29% when jointly regularizing both encoder and diffusion training stages.

### Your Transformer Can Hold Two Thoughts at Once: Evidence of Linear Superposition in LLMs
**78 upvotes** · Tikhonov et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2609.29845)

This paper establishes the "Superposition Linearity Hypothesis": when two input text streams are linearly combined, a Transformer's output is approximately a linear superposition of the individual next-token distributions. Interestingly, this property appears to be architectural rather than learned — it actually *weakens* during pretraining — but can be restored via lightweight fine-tuning. The authors also introduce a guided decoding method that disentangles superposed outputs, enabling two coherent continuations to be generated simultaneously from a single forward pass, with potential implications for parallel decoding and mechanistic interpretability.

## 🛠 Tools

### vectorize-io/hindsight — Agent Memory That Learns
**4,413 stars/day** · 40,044 total stars · Python · [Repo](https://github.com/vectorize-io/hindsight)

Hindsight is an open-source (MIT) agent memory system that goes beyond conversation history recall — it lets agents extract and generalize knowledge from past interactions to improve future behavior. It ships with Python and NPM clients, an integration catalog, benchmarks, and a hosted cloud option. The distinction from vanilla RAG-based memory is the focus on *learning* patterns over time rather than just retrieving verbatim history.

### debpalash/VoiceStudio — Fully-local ElevenLabs alternative
**3,274 stars/day** · Python · [Repo](https://github.com/debpalash/VoiceStudio)

VoiceStudio is an open-source, fully local voice toolkit covering voice cloning, voice design, video dubbing, dictation, transcription, and audiobook creation across 646 languages — with no API calls or cloud costs. The direct positioning against ElevenLabs is significant: it offers comparable functionality for teams with data-privacy requirements or cost constraints. Running entirely on-device also makes it viable for air-gapped environments.

### paperclipai/paperclip — Open-source agent orchestration for teams
**3,185 stars/day** · 91,943 total stars · TypeScript · [Repo](https://github.com/paperclipai/paperclip)

Paperclip is a Node.js + React platform that treats AI agents like employees in a company: you define goals, assign agents, set budgets, and monitor work and costs from a single dashboard — org charts, governance, and goal alignment included. The framing ("if OpenClaw is an employee, Paperclip is the company") captures its scope: it's not an agent framework but an *orchestration layer* over existing agents. With ~92K stars it has clearly struck a nerve as enterprises look for ways to manage fleets of autonomous agents responsibly.

### Quoting Muse AI Agent
*2026-09-28* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Sep/28/muse-ai-agent/) · tags: `meta`, `generative-ai`, `muse-agent`, `ai`, `general-agents`, `llms`

Simon highlights a real-world example of an AI agent (Muse) acting autonomously on a user's behalf for a Marketplace pickup — and causing harm when it sent an auto-reply falsely confirming the seller was home. The agent then proactively identified the failure, sent an apology from the user's account, and proposed a policy fix. This is a sharp, concrete illustration of the agentic failure mode where an LLM's confident output causes real-world damage (a negative rating, a broken transaction) even when the agent correctly diagnoses the mistake after the fact.
