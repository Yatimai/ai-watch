## 🔬 Research

### EnvHarness: Awakening Static Worlds for Agent Learning
**257 upvotes** · Chengsong Huang, Zifeng Wang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.19880)

Training environments for LLM agents are typically hand-built and static — they don't adapt as agents improve. EnvHarness introduces a plug-in wrapper layer that reshapes existing environments without touching their underlying logic, and EnvRigger automates the process by observing agent trajectories to diagnose weaknesses and synthesize targeted variants. Across 5 benchmarks in 4 domains, the approach yields up to +9.0 points on held-out instances with 9.8% fewer execution steps. The ability to reuse existing verifiers while continuously co-evolving the policy and its environment is a practical step toward scalable, curriculum-style RL training.

### FACET: Preserving Source Intent and Executable State in Terminal Task Synthesis
**115 upvotes** · Kou Shi, Zun Wang et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.18580)

Generating high-quality training tasks for terminal agents is hard: instruction, environment state, reference solution, and verifier must all be mutually consistent, yet multi-stage generation tends to drop critical context. FACET reconstructs agent skills into coherent scenarios, initialises and repairs the execution environment first, then derives the remaining artefacts from that shared container state — ensuring alignment by construction. Models fine-tuned on FACET-generated tasks improve consistently across scales on Terminal-Bench 2.1. The two design principles it validates — source-intent preservation and shared executable-state grounding — are directly applicable to anyone building synthetic data pipelines for agentic code tasks.

### Co-RL: Unsupervised Reasoning Emerges from Diverse Cohort in Multi-agent RL
**92 upvotes** · Yunhao Yang, Yuexin Bian et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2608.17253) · [Code](https://github.com/DrStranded/Co-RL)

Ground-truth labels for reasoning tasks are expensive and increasingly scarce; self-rewarding RL is an alternative but tends toward homogenization and collapse. Co-RL trains a cohort of decoupled models (no shared parameters) that reward each other, with deliberate diversity across model families, sizes, and rephrased prompts to break correlated feedback loops. Without any ground-truth labels, Co-RL gains 3.0–8.6% on seven text-only benchmarks and 2.3–7.2% on four multimodal benchmarks over baselines, matching or beating supervised methods. This is a credible path to scaling reasoning improvement once human annotations become the bottleneck.

*2026-08-23* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/23/anthropics-best-ai-model-struggles-to-attract-users-as-cheaper-t/) · tags: `anthropic`, `claude`, `claude-mythos-fable`, `llms`, `openai`

Simon aggregates FT numbers: Anthropic's annualized revenue hit $65B in July (up from $47B in May), yet its top model **Fable 5** captures only 8% of Anthropic spend according to Ramp billing data from 70,000 companies — far behind Opus 4.8 at 28%. ### Drew Breunig — Fable & The End of the Free Lunch
*2026-08-23* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/23/drew-breunig/) · tags: `anthropic`, `claude-mythos-fable`, `llm-pricing`

Breunig articulates a shift in engineering calculus: before Fable, cheap/better models kept arriving fast enough that investing in prompt engineering or retrieval harnesses was rarely worth it. Fable broke that pattern — it's genuinely more capable, but expensive enough that teams now have to reason carefully about *which* tasks justify the cost. This is directly relevant to anyone building agentic coding workflows: the era of "wait for the next model to fix it" may be over, making tool-level optimization (like the skills repos above) more economically justified.

### Linus Torvalds on AI-Assisted Debugging
*2026-08-22* · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/22/linus-torvalds/) · tags: `linus-torvalds`, `ai-assisted-programming`, `linux`

In a Linux kernel commit message, Torvalds credits an AI with handling "much of the grunt-work" in a difficult debug session on the `drm/xe` driver — but notes the model repeatedly declared the problem "impossible and unsolvable" before being pushed to continue. The anecdote illustrates both the genuine utility of AI for low-level debugging and a concrete limitation: models may be calibrated toward giving up when human experts would persist. Worth bearing in mind for long-horizon agentic debugging tasks.
