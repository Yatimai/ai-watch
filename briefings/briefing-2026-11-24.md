### Embo-Harness: Towards Understanding Frontier-Model-as-Policy Harness
**89 upvotes** · Xiao-Ming Wu et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.31201)

Embo-Harness is a framework that studies how to best wire frontier multimodal models directly as robot policies inside closed-loop control loops, examining four key components: prompting, planning, action execution, and tool use. The core finding is that leaning on the model's native visual reasoning and tool-use beats hand-crafted skill libraries or trajectory priors. On the RoboLab-120 benchmark it reaches 83.3% success rate (+26.2 pp over the best baseline), and succeeds on 15 of 20 zero-shot real-world manipulation trials. Notably, accurate spatial target estimation matters more than explicit joint/trajectory generation — a useful design constraint for practitioners building robot harnesses today.

### WorldLeague: Benchmarking Physical Agency
**70 upvotes** · Xinyang Zhou, Qi Li, Xinchao Wang · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30989)

WorldLeague is a 60-task simulated benchmark that evaluates AI systems (VLMs, VLAs, and world-action models) on physical agency across six capability dimensions: scene exploration, object rearrangement, and adaptation to changes. It introduces an **Interactive Score** alongside binary success rate to measure *partial* progress toward goals — a more informative signal than pass/fail alone. Among 13 tested systems, Claude Fable 5.2 leads with 13/60 goals achieved and a mean score of 28.58/100; even top VLMs score 0 on 71.8% of episodes they answer correctly, exposing a deep gap between language reasoning and embodied execution. This benchmark gives the embodied AI community a unified comparison surface that works across heterogeneous model architectures.

### WeMM-Ecom: Advancing Multimodal Content Understanding for WeChat E-Commerce
**54 upvotes** · Binhui Xie et al. (Tencent) · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2611.30662)

WeMM-Ecom is a family of five multimodal LLMs (0.8B dense → 35B MoE with 3B active params) purpose-built for e-commerce tasks, trained with two-stage continued pre-training plus hybrid-thinking post-training (RL + on-policy distillation). The 35B-A3B model outperforms much larger open-source models like Qwen3.8-Flash-Next and DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash on a 28-benchmark e-commerce suite, while every size variant beats its Qwen3.5 counterpart on 64 general-purpose benchmarks. It is already live in WeChat powering catalog management, product search, and livestream commerce at **billion-scale requests per day**. Weights and code are released at [github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Ecom](https://github.com/Tencent/WeMM-Ecom).

### HKUDS/CLI-Anything — Make any software agent-native via auto-generated CLIs
**1,075 stars/day** · ⭐ 64,569 total · [Repo](https://github.com/HKUDS/CLI-Anything)

CLI-Anything automatically generates structured command-line interfaces for arbitrary software so that AI agents can interact with them natively, bridging the gap between today's GUI-centric apps and agent-driven workflows. It ships with a community hub (`pip install cli-anything-hub` + `cli-hub install <name>`) for sharing and discovering generated CLIs. The premise is timely: as agentic systems proliferate, the lack of machine-friendly interfaces for most software is a real bottleneck, and CLI-Anything tackles it at the layer where it hurts.

## 📡 Analysis

### GPT 6.2 Astra Solves a Historical Mystery
2026-11-24 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Nov/24/astra-historical-mystery/) · tags: `history`, `ai`, `generative-ai`, `llms`, `ai-assisted-search`, `deep-blue`, `gpt-6-astra`

History professor Mark Humphries used GPT-6.2 Astra to autonomously identify the creator of an anonymous 1808 sketchbook held at Yale's Beinecke Library — in just **22 minutes**, using only public sources. The model located an obscure diary reference, predicted that a physical archive at Cleveland State University would confirm its hypothesis, and was proven right, surfacing a document of independent historical significance. Humphries draws a measured comparison to chess's Deep Blue moment, suggesting historians will face a "crisis of confidence" by spring/summer — but a milder one than mathematicians, since historians interpret an evolving present rather than chase a single truth.
