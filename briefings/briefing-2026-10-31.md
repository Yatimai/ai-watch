## 🔬 Research

### Intrinsic Distribution Matching (IDM)
**241 upvotes** · Deyuan Liu et al. (inclusionAI) · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.39633)

IDM improves one-step and few-step text-to-image distillation by adding explicit moment constraints (feature mean + per-coordinate std) on top of standard Distribution Matching Distillation (DMD). Crucially, it uses the generator's own EMA copy to extract features, removing the need for an external encoder like DINOv2. Tested on Z-Image-Turbo 6B and Qwen-Image 20B, IDM achieves **94.25% / 90.20%** on Chinese/English LongText-Bench at just two network evaluations — the best in class at that inference budget. Code is open-sourced at [github.com/inclusionAI/IDM](https://github.com/inclusionAI/IDM).

### A-RSI: Adaptive Task Refresh for Zero-Data Multimodal Self-Improvement
**160 upvotes** · Yu Liu et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.38568)

A-RSI tackles a blind spot in recursive self-improvement: knowing *when* to stop mining a task distribution, not just *what* to generate next. It monitors reward variation and accuracy in training logs to automatically refresh the task pool when the learning signal decays, using no external examples or human labels. Answers are verified independently via model-authored programs, sidestepping majority-voting's circular dependency on the model's own uncertain outputs. On an 11-benchmark suite, A-RSI lifts the average score from **57.77 to 61.05** with only 800 freshly generated tasks.

### Your Benchmark Is an Empirical Measure Over Difficulty
**144 upvotes** · Yifan Sun et al. · [Paper](https://huggingface.co/papers/2610.29927)

This paper reframes benchmark evaluation as a measure over difficulty, exposing two systematic failure modes: *measure mismatch* (wrong difficulty weighting) and *coverage gaps* (blind spots along the difficulty axis). An analysis of **25 popular benchmarks across 7 domains** shows that many implicitly over-represent certain difficulty regions, making leaderboard rankings misleading. The authors introduce BenchFill, a difficulty-targeted item rewriting algorithm to patch coverage gaps — a practically useful tool for anyone designing or interpreting evals.

## 📡 Analysis

### dbreunig/nesformer — A working NES CPU emulator baked into Qwen3.5-4B
2026-10-30 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/30/nesformer/) · tags: `games`, `ai`, `llms`, `qwen`, `conformance-suites`

Drew Breunig distilled a functional emulator for the NES 6502 CPU directly into the weights of Qwen3.5-4B, inspired by the earlier `qwenputer` experiment. The result: you can actually run NES ROMs (including Super Mario Bros) inside a language model — slowly, but correctly. The project used Opus 5.5 for development and validated correctness against a 15-year-old NES CPU conformance test suite, which is a surprisingly rigorous approach for what is essentially a party trick.

### Building a $3M/month autonomous AI game studio at Spawn
2026-10-29 · [Post](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Oct/29/how-i-built-the-most-ai-pilled-company-in-the-world/) · tags: `agentic-engineering`, `claude`, `coding-agents`, `game-design`

Jacob Sansbury details how Spawn — a multiplayer game world where players prompt the environment into existence — is now autonomously developed by a swarm of Fable-powered agents burning **$3M/month in tokens** (30× their payroll). The agents read production errors, triage bugs (many filed by Savi, the in-game AI), and ship fixes to production **hourly**, with a missed hour treated as a SEV-0 incident. Sansbury credits Fable 5/5.1 as the model capability threshold that made this level of autonomy viable, noting that GPT-6 Astra broke everything when tried for security fixes — a rare, candid model comparison from a production system.
